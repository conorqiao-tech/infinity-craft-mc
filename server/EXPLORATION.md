# Exploration and scores — 2.13.0-explore.26

**Infinity Journey.** Open **Infinity Menu → Infinity Journey** for six paths linking the Expedition Atlas, Odyssey ruins, Astral guardians, Builder Exchange, courses and Commons pantry. Existing progress counts. Attune a completed path to wear its optional trail; attune all six for the Constellation trail and an original sigil banner. The banner can be requested again and goes into an empty inventory slot. Trails can be turned off, are hidden while invisible, and viewers can hide other players' Journey trails. The journey does not replace blocks or existing items. Bedrock uses a native form; its particle and banner appearance still needs a real-device check.

**Infinity Ore Survey** is an optional Survival/Hardcore mining challenge in Infinity Journey. Start it and break each of eight ore types; normal and deepslate variants count together, and Nether gold counts as gold. Player-placed ores count as well. Pausing keeps progress, and completing the survey unlocks a repeatable decorative banner in an empty slot. The survey does not change ordinary drops, experience or the block-breaking rules.

**Geode bonus.** While the Ore Survey is active, harvest a fully grown amethyst cluster to mark a bonus discovery in the survey. Player-placed clusters count. Buds can keep growing, and the banner still requires the original eight ores.

**Infinity Wheat Harvest.** In **Infinity Journey**, choose **Neighbor • Commons pantry** to open **Infinity Harvest Together**, then select **Start Infinity Wheat Harvest**. Harvest eight fully grown wheat crops in Survival or Hardcore. Each ripe crop counts once when you break it, and the same menu keeps your progress between sessions. Collect your wheat normally or offer it through a pantry delivery.

**Infinity Star Loom.** Open **Infinity Menu → Infinity Journey → Infinity Star Loom** and pick Kite, Half Moon or Ember Peaks. Match the nine lights to the target. Flipping one also flips its neighbors directly above, below, left and right. On Java the gold target is on the left and the playable board is on the right. On Bedrock read the two grids row by row, then tap a coordinate such as **Flip A2** for the top middle star. Each chart keeps your board and fewest moves. Solving reveals a matching banner recipe. Take the listed wool, dyes and stick to a crafting table and use one ingredient per slot in any order. Replay to beat your own score.

**Star Loom Banner Concert.** Hang or stand the Kite, Half Moon and Ember Peaks banners in your build with space overhead for the lights. Hold an amethyst shard in your main hand and use it on a design to play a chime and see its stars above the banner. The shard is reusable. Improvise in free play, or open **Infinity Journey → Infinity Star Loom → Banner Concert** and pick Moon Dance, Cinder Waltz or Infinity Refrain. Follow the next banner shown above your hotbar. A wrong note lets you retry that cue. Finish for a gold-and-teal infinity flourish, or play every cue cleanly to mark the phrase **Flawless**. Each player hears and sees their own performance.

**Echo Challenge.** In Banner Concert free play, choose **Echo Challenge → Start Echo Challenge**. Tap one Kite, one Half Moon and one Ember Peaks with your shard to link the three banners. Stand where you can see them. Each beat lights a constellation above its banner and names it above your hotbar. When **Your turn** appears, play the same banners in order. Each correct tap fills a circle. Take your time. A miss repeats the same call, and **Replay this call** lets you hear it again. Each cleared round adds one note to the previous call. Match all five for the infinity flourish. The menu remembers your best round.

**Mirror Echo.** Choose **Start Mirror Echo** on the Echo Challenge page and link the same three banner designs. When the hotbar says **Your turn • Reverse order**, start with the last banner you heard. If the call was Kite, Half Moon, Ember Peaks, tap Ember Peaks, Half Moon, Kite. Your first correct tap lights a star. Each following correct tap sends a silver arc from the previous banner to this one. A miss or replay keeps the call. Mirror Echo saves its own best across three-, four-, and five-note rounds.

**Mirror Composer.** Open **Banner Concert → Echo Challenge → Mirror Composer**. Add two to six notes with the named banner buttons. Repeats are allowed. Each choice sounds its note, and **Undo last note** plays back the shortened melody. The score saves as you edit. Select **Perform on my banners**, then tap one banner of each design in your score with an amethyst shard. Try Kite, Half Moon, Ember Peaks. You will hear Kite, Half Moon, Ember Peaks, Half Moon, Kite. A gold ring and a vertical line of stars mark the turning note. After a brief hold, silver arcs lead back home. Your performance is private. You can perform the saved melody at another stage by linking its banners.

**Star Prints.** Write at least two notes, hold plain paper, then choose **Mirror Composer → Star Prints → Print my draft**. One sheet becomes a print bearing your name and the melody as it sounded when printed. Give it to another player or keep it in a chest. Hold the print and use it, or open **Star Prints** from Mirror Composer. **Unfold held print** plays the written notes and draws a private constellation from left to right in front of you. Higher pitches sit higher, and repeated notes get separate stars. **Perform held print** plays the paper out and back after you link its banner designs with an amethyst shard. You can use the print again. Each player's own draft is saved separately.

**Score Gallery.** Place a Star Print into an ordinary or glow item frame. Use an amethyst shard on the displayed print to hear its melody and see the stars connect across its face. Crouch and use the shard to trace the same stars from the last note to the first. On touch, turn on Sneak before tapping the frame. The arrow and composer’s name above your hotbar identify the piece and direction. Each visitor hears their own performance, so two people can play the same exhibit in opposite directions.

**Gallery Trail.** Use a plain compass on any framed Star Print to start. Read the ending note above your hotbar, then find a different frame whose score opens with that note. Each frame can be up to 12 blocks from the one before it. Try **Kite → Half Moon**, then **Half Moon → Ember Peaks**, then **Ember Peaks → Kite**. Each accepted score plays for you. White sparks join the frames, and three gold halos mark the finished constellation at the end of the third melody. A wrong guess keeps your place. Crouch and use the compass on a print to start a fresh trail there. Leaving the dimension or signing out clears your trail.

**Trail Encore.** Finish a Gallery Trail and use a plain feather on its last frame to conduct all three prints as one medley. On touch, turn on Sneak before tapping that same frame to conduct backward. When two scores meet, their shared note sounds once. Both frames flash in the note’s color and a white arc connects them. Repeats written inside a score keep their own beats. You can replay the encore until you start a new trail, leave the dimension, or sign out. The three original prints must still be on display.

**Encore Duet.** Two players solve the same Gallery Trail in the same order. Hold plain sticks and tap the last frame within 30 seconds of one another. The first player leads with a chime and the second answers with a bell. Take turns tapping the frame named above your hotbar, one tap per note. A bar marks the lead’s frame and a hollow diamond marks the reply’s. Both players hear the whole performance. A wrong tap keeps the beat waiting. On the final note, all three frames light up for the pair. To leave, turn on Sneak and tap a trail frame with your stick.

**Duet Coda.** After the last beat, each performer can choose a note from any of the three prints with a plain glow ink sac. Tap for the print’s closing note. Turn on Sneak and tap for its opening note. The first performer can change their choice while the other decides. When both have picked, the chime and bell sound together, and an infinity sign in the notes’ colors lights up between the frames. Pick the same frame to draw both loops on its face. Only the pair sees and hears the chord. Pick again to try another combination. The ink is reusable.

**Coda Postcards.** While the pair is still together, either performer can tap the last trail frame with plain paper to stamp the latest completed chord. Each copy costs one sheet and carries both performers’ names and pitches. Give a card to another player or keep it for yourself. Hold it and use it in the air to listen, or choose **Play held postcard** under **Mirror Composer → Star Prints**. A shimmer on the left shows the chime and a gold ring on the right shows the bell. Higher pitches sit higher, and a unison puts the two shapes level. Only the reader sees and hears the replay.

**Postcard Gallery.** Place a stamped postcard in an item frame or glow item frame. Tap it with a plain amethyst shard to play the signed chord. Turn on Sneak and tap again to hear the chime and bell trade notes. Their colored bar and ring follow the face of the display, including floor and ceiling frames. Each visitor hears their own arrangement, and the original card keeps its signed pitches.

**Postcard Pairs.** Use a plain echo shard on a framed postcard to hear its voices. Choose a second framed postcard within 12 blocks whose chime matches the first card’s bell. Sneak on either tap to swap that card’s voices. For example, start with Kite → Half Moon, then Sneak-tap Ember Peaks → Half Moon. The link flashes at Half Moon while Kite and Ember Peaks sound together. Two colored threads cross between the frames for you alone. A mismatch keeps your first choice. Tap that first card again to clear it.

**Postcard Circuit.** After linking two postcards, use plain prismarine crystals on a third signed frame. Its chime must match the second card’s bell and its bell must return to the first card’s chime. All three displays must be within 12 blocks of one another. Sneak to swap the third card for this tap. For the Kite → Half Moon → Ember Peaks pair above, finish on Ember Peaks → Kite. Three voices sound together as colored edges connect your frames. The return edge bows away from a straight row of frames. Count the sparks at each corner to follow the route; a hollow diamond marks a completed circuit. Only you see and hear it. A wrong third card lets you retry. Starting a new echo-shard pair replaces the old link.

**Circuit Conductor.** Close a Postcard Circuit, then use a plain blaze rod on any of its three frames. That frame sets the opening beat. Tap the next two corners and return to the opening frame. Sneak on the first tap to go backward. For example, start on the harp at corner 2 and Sneak: play **2 → 1 → 3 → 2**. The first three beats play the individual voices, and the fourth plays the full chord. Follow the hollow diamond to the next frame and the ring with a bright center back home. Four marks along the lower frame edge fill in as you play. A wrong corner gives you the same cue again. There is no timer or item cost. Pick a different opening for another phrase, or close a new circuit to change the three voices.

**Circuit Counterpoint.** Finish a Conductor phrase, then tap its frames with a plain copper ingot. Answer each beat one corner ahead of the lead, following the direction you performed. If you conducted **2 → 1 → 3 → 2**, answer **1 → 3 → 2 → 1**. The lead frame lights up with a bar and your answer with a ring as their two voices sound together. On the fourth beat all three frames flash a three-ray star and play the full chord. A miss names the next corner and keeps your place. Play the harmony again, finish a different phrase to change it, or close a fresh circuit to start over. Only you see and hear the performance. The ingot stays in your hand.

**Infinity Chord Seal.** Finish the four copper answers, then use a plain clay ball on any of the three signed frames. One ball becomes a portable copy of the phrase. Give it to another player or use it in the air to listen, one beat per tap. Sneak-use plays the first beat again. A floating medallion points to each beat, and its center lights up on the final triad. The clay remembers its original pitches and direction even after the gallery changes. Each listener keeps their own place.

**Seal Cipher.** Use any stamped Chord Seal on a note block to start a private three-round puzzle. Each clue plays two instruments from your seal. Identify the one that stayed silent: chime, harp or bell. The menu names the two sounding voices and shows one empty socket, so you can solve it muted. Replay a clue as often as you like. A wrong choice keeps your place; solving the third round sounds the full triad. A gifted seal works even after its original frames are gone. The puzzle leaves the note block’s tuning and your normal playback position alone.

**Cipher Refrain.** After the third clue, choose Forward refrain to play the missing instruments in clue order, or Backward refrain to play them in reverse. The first two correct choices sound their solo pitches. The last ties the three knots and sounds the whole seal together. A miss keeps your place. Reveal next voice gives a silent hint at any step. You can play either direction again with the same seal.

**Refrain Cadence.** Finish a forward or backward refrain and choose Weave a cadence. Pick any numbered knot to hear its solo, then answer with the two other instruments together. If you pick a pair containing the soloist, you can try again from the same call. The thread forks when the duet answers. Play full chord adds all three voices on demand. Choose Weave again to try a different soloist.

**Cadence Pennant.** After the duet answers, choose Weave pennant to trade one plain white banner from your hotbar or main inventory for a placeable keepsake. The soloist sets the center mark: a vertical stripe for chime, a diagonal for harp, or a roundel for bell. Read the side stripes in chime–harp–bell order starting just after the soloist. Their colors record the answering charts: cyan for Kite, purple for Half Moon and orange for Ember Peaks. Each completed cadence can weave one pennant. Play another cadence to make another.

**Pennant Fanfare.** Hold a plain feather and tap a placed Cadence Pennant to sound its solo. Tap the same flag again to hear its two side stripes answer together. Sneak-tap to repeat the call. A different flag starts its own call, even when its pattern looks identical. The performer alone receives the notes, particle marks and instrument captions. Standing, wall-mounted, gifted and identically hand-woven pennants all work from the three visible layers.

**Pennant Chorus.** After hearing a flag’s answer, use plain paper on that same flag to collect its center solo. Gather one chime, one harp and one bell in any order. Collecting another flag with the same instrument replaces that pitch. The third instrument sounds the chord you assembled and lights a triangle at the flag. Each player has a private chorus for the session. The next collection starts a new trio. You keep the paper.

**Chorus Baton.** On the flag where you finished your chorus, tap a plain stick to play the solos in the order you first collected the instruments. A replacement pitch keeps its original place. Sneak on the first tap to conduct backward. Each tap plays one beat: three solos, then all three voices together. A white stroke points to the active corner. After the chord you can choose either direction again. Collecting the first solo of another trio retires the old score.

**Infinity Loop.** Finish a forward baton phrase to light the left arc above the flag. Finish a backward phrase to light the right. Either can come first. When the arcs meet, tap the same flag with a plain amethyst shard to ring the first and last instruments you collected together. The shard is reusable, including between baton beats. A fresh trio starts with an empty loop.

**Loop Inlay.** Ring your completed loop with an amethyst shard. The duet leaves out the instrument you collected second. Use plain glowstone dust on a placed Cadence Pennant whose solo matches that instrument and its last collected pitch. Keep the finishing flag in the loaded area. A wrong guess gives a hint. A match sounds the three collected voices and sets a diamond in the loop at the answering flag. You keep the dust and your baton beat. Ring again to play another round.

**Inlay Prism.** After setting the diamond, hold plain nether quartz and use it on your original finishing flag. Each solved inlay yields one prism and costs one quartz. A stack needs an empty inventory slot for the remaining quartz. Carry the prism away or give it to another player. Use it to light the left facet and hear the first and middle instruments. Sneak-use lights the right facet and plays the middle and last instruments. Each facet keeps the pitches that were collected when you cut it.

**Prism Crossing.** Use a cut prism on an amethyst block to see two instrument clues. Sneak at the block to reverse their order. Aim into the air and play the facet containing each outside instrument: use for left or sneak-use for right. The first match lights a diagonal and plays a duet. The second crosses it with another diagonal, lights a star and plays the full chord. A miss keeps your place. Tap the block again to start over.

**Lantern Walk.** Finish a Prism Crossing, then touch three different ordinary lanterns with that prism. Each new lantern must be within 12 blocks of every earlier stop in the same dimension. The stops play the prism’s instruments one at a time, following the direction of the crossing. The third closes a constellation between the lanterns. Each player sees their own lights. Start another crossing to walk again.

**Star Survey.** After the third lantern, find the longest span between two of your stops, counting height as well as horizontal distance. Sneak-use your prism on the lantern opposite that span. If two spans tie, either opposite lantern works. A miss keeps the survey open. A correct sighting sounds the two endpoint instruments together, lights the span and draws a compass rose above your viewpoint. Each walk earns one survey.

**Midpoint Beacon.** After a correct sighting, use the same prism on a clear glass block one block above the midpoint of your chosen span. For a midpoint between block centers, either nearest coordinate works on each axis, including height. A miss gives a direction to adjust. A hit lights two private rays and a halo at the glass and plays the observing lantern’s solo. Each survey lights one beacon.

**Beacon Relay.** Use the prism on the two lanterns at the ends of that span without sneaking. Start at the one closer to the glass you lit, including its height. If both are equally close, either can lead. The first lantern plays its solo and lights one arch. Visiting the other plays the full chord once and traces a figure eight above your glass. A wrong lantern gives a silent clue and keeps your place.

**Ribbon Return.** After finishing Beacon Relay, sneak-use the same prism on the exact glass you lit. The lantern that finished the relay sounds again, and a dotted arc returns to your glass. Use the observing lantern without sneaking to hear its solo. Then use the lantern that led your relay. Both endpoints answer together and a four-point star marks the center of your infinity ribbon. Wrong guesses keep your place. On a tied relay, remember which lantern you chose first.

**Star Lens.** After Ribbon Return, place a full stained-glass block directly on top of the clear glass you lit. Use the same prism on the colored block. Match the chart of the observing lantern, the voice missing from the closing duet: cyan for Kite, purple for Half Moon, or orange for Ember Peaks. The correct lens plays that lantern’s solo and projects its original nine-cell Star Loom pattern above the glass. Use the matching lens again to see the chart silently. Your blocks stay in place.

**Star Trace.** Look at the projected board and count the bright stars in each column. The first, second and third lantern you visited correspond to the left, middle and right columns. Use your prism on a lantern for a column with the most stars. Either tied column works. Its lantern plays its own solo, and two lines appear beneath the chosen column. Use that lantern or the matching lens again for a silent redraw. Keep the tinted lens on the original clear glass.

**Skyprint.** After marking a column, use a sheet of plain paper on the matching stained glass above your original beacon glass. Each press costs one sheet. With a stack, keep a spare inventory slot for the remaining blanks. Your print records the observer’s constellation, your marked column and that lantern’s solo. Carry it or pass it to a friend. Use the print in the air to see the chart and play the solo. Sneak-use it in the air for a silent view.



**Infinity Glassworks.** At a crafting table, place eight matching glass panes in eight different slots. The empty slot can be anywhere. Take three full blocks of the same glass. Clear panes and all sixteen stained colors work. Picking up any of these panes unlocks the Glassworks recipes in the Java recipe book. To cut panes again, the ordinary recipe turns six blocks into sixteen panes. The Builder Exchange guide in Infinity Journey also lists the recipe.

**Infinity Chimes.** Tune a note block, aim at it, then open **Infinity Menu → Infinity Journey → Infinity Chimes**. Choose **Copy** to hear the chime and add the note to your personal score. Note names follow the standard harp octave. Copy up to eight notes in order, including repeats. **Play my score** plays the saved pitches privately in a chime voice, eight game ticks apart. You can listen away from the original blocks. **Undo last note** removes one note at a time. The score stays with your character when you leave and come back.

**Infinity Apiary.** Aim at a bee nest or beehive and open **Infinity Menu → Infinity Journey → Infinity Apiary** to read its honey level and check whether campfire smoke reaches it. At level 5/5, a smoked hive is ready for harvest. Choose **Start Apiary Keeper** in Survival or Hardcore, close the guide, then use a glass bottle on a full smoked hive for honey and shears on a full smoked hive for comb. Each harvest empties the hive, so use another full hive or let the same one refill. Either order counts. The guide saves both entries with your character.

**Infinity Tidewatch.** Open **Infinity Menu → Infinity Journey → Infinity Tidewatch** to record cod, salmon, tropical fish and pufferfish. In Survival or Hardcore, select the species while a living fish is in water within eight blocks with a clear view. A clear glass aquarium window lets you record a fish through it. Each species fills one entry in your personal journal, and you can return later to finish the collection.

**Tidewatch signal flags.** Each recorded fish unlocks a banner design. Aim at a loom, open **Infinity Tidewatch → Signal flags**, and choose **Weave** in Survival or Hardcore. Each flag uses one plain white banner and one dye of each color shown. Shoal uses cyan and blue, Upstream uses red and gray, Reef uses orange and light blue, and Caution uses black and yellow. Carry the supplies in your hotbar or main inventory. Place the woven flag on the ground or hang it on a wall like any banner.

**Personal trailhead.** Open the **Expedition Atlas** in Survival or Hardcore and choose **Mark my trailhead here**. The Atlas saves the block coordinates and dimension with your character. Open the Atlas again or choose **Refresh trailhead bearing** for a compass direction and approximate horizontal distance back to that spot. In another dimension the Atlas shows where you left it. Use **Replace my saved trailhead with this location** to move your mark.

**Infinity Defender** is an optional Survival/Hardcore combat challenge in Infinity Journey. Start it and personally defeat one of each of eight hostile families. Spawned hostiles can count. Pausing keeps progress, and all eight unlock a repeatable decorative banner in an empty slot. This path does not select targets or alter creatures, helpers, damage or drops.

Twenty-one biome palettes dress the original circuit shape, with one chest holding the lower chest's exact loot and its original book. A matching starting prophecy arrives in an empty inventory slot; recover it through **Infinity Menu → The Odyssey • your prophecy**. **Faster menu and inventory gear.** OP4 can choose **Infinity Menu → My stats • quick edit**, select a stat and enter its exact value. Bedrock/iPad uses a native number field and a separate Confirm button. Java uses **Enter exact number**, private chat input (no slash), then Review and Confirm. The number is private and the prompt expires after 90 seconds; `cancel` discards it. Manual stat edits need only the admin’s own confirmation; Codex approval is for AI orders. Health regeneration or hunger changes no longer cancel an approved absolute vitals edit. Plus/minus buttons update the same Java screen. Normal combat attribute caps are removed only for edited instances, so Health capacity can exceed 1024 and damage/armor can exceed their normal attribute caps. Reset restores the original base and normal cap. Entering health or absorption above its current capacity now includes the required capacity increase in the same confirmation. Absorption saves and reloads its remaining amount. Armor above 30 now affects native armor reduction, up to full armor protection; armor-bypassing damage and native weapon effects still apply. Movement, size, loot and interaction retain native engine/client limits, and XP fits vanilla's total-experience integer. Food, saturation and exhaustion accept larger native reserves as one-time edits; ordinary gameplay can consume or reset them, and clients may cap their bars.

They use dyed leather armor on Bedrock, including native icons and wearable assets regardless of Java pack state. The Infinity winged chestplate is represented by an elytra on Bedrock.
